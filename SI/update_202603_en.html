October 2026 Data Update

Updated findings on research cited by policy documents, using Overton 2026-10-04 and OpenAlex 2026-06-25.

English baseline summary · Full Korean update · 2025 Korean report PDF

Updated: 8 October 2026 · Overton: 2026-10-04 · OpenAlex: 2026-06-25

English text revised: 12 October 2026. The data and calculations are unchanged.

Note

This page presents the updated online analysis. The linked report PDF and baseline supplements describe the 2025 edition. The analysis covers observed citations from policy documents to research linked through DOIs. DOIs that map to more than one OpenAlex work are excluded.

Korea’s two citation directions

We compare policy sources that cite Korean research with the research cited by Korean policy documents. Research country refers to the first author’s institutional affiliation, not nationality. Here, a work is an OpenAlex research record linked through a DOI; the sample is not restricted to journal articles.

The top three policy source categories—the United States, international organizations, and South Korea—account for 49.7% of citations to Korean research, counted by source category. Among citations from Korean policy documents with an assigned research country, U.S. research accounts for 45.4% and Korean research for 3.9%. These percentages describe different citation directions and use different denominators. Policy source categories can overlap, so counts by source category can differ from the number of distinct citations.

Research country is unassigned for 109,309 of 418,179 citations from Korean policy documents (26.1%). If all unassigned citations were placed in the non-U.S. or U.S. category, the U.S. share among all citations in this direction would range from 33.5% to 59.7%. These are mechanical bounds, not confidence intervals. The reported 45.4% uses only the 308,870 citations with an assigned research country.

Comparing observed and expected country shares

Both shares use the same 308,842 citations from Korean policy documents with a research country and domain assigned. Of the citations with an assigned research country, 28 have no domain and are excluded from this comparison.

To calculate the expected share, we split each citation’s weight equally across its assigned domains, giving each citation a total weight of one. We then combine this domain mix with the country distribution of unique cited works within each domain of the linked sample. Only works with an assigned research country contribute to that reference distribution.

Among these citations, the observed share of U.S. research is 45.4% and the expected share is 35.1%, a difference of 10.3 percentage points. The expected share is a descriptive reference based on the linked sample’s field and country composition. It does not measure total OpenAlex research output or specify a policy target.

Figure 1 uses all citations with an assigned research country, including those without domains. The alternative calculations, domain weights, country distributions within domains and comparison sample counts document how the two shares were calculated.

We divide policy documents into two groups that do not overlap: documents with a top-level think-tank tag, and all other documents. Documents without a source type belong to the second group; the number of citations from such documents in these two directions is 0. Each group’s weight is its share of domain assignments within that direction.

We split the 41.1 percentage-point social-science gap into two descriptive components. Differences in the mix of source groups account for 16.6 percentage points in this calculation; differences in social-science shares within the groups account for 24.5 percentage points. These are arithmetic components, not estimates of causal effects.

The calculation treats the two directions symmetrically. For each group, the composition component multiplies the difference in group weights by the average social-science share. The within-group component multiplies the difference in social-science shares by the average group weight. Both averages are taken across the two directions, and the components are summed across groups. Components by source group.

Data linkage and comparability

  • 8,386,474 of 8,767,042 unique DOIs match OpenAlex. The 104,964 DOIs mapping to multiple work IDs are excluded from the primary analysis; an unrestricted-mapping sensitivity table is available.
  • The first author’s affiliation determines research country. We select the ROR country code that occurs most often across affiliation records; ties are broken alphabetically by country code. When no ROR country is available, we use an OpenAlex institution country only if the available codes agree on a single country. Works that cannot be assigned a country remain unassigned.
  • The 2025 baseline used a different fallback field. Snapshot coverage, DOI disambiguation and the fallback field all changed, so differences from the baseline must not be interpreted directly as behavioral trends.
  • Recent works were published in 2023–2025. Their share is calculated among cited works with a known publication year through 2025. The incomplete 2026 publication year is excluded. The baseline instead used 2022–2024.
  • Policy-source tags may overlap; citation counts do not measure causal policy impact.
