Note

This is a web-only English summary. The report manuscript and detailed SI are provided in Korean. It includes only the definitions needed to read the English summary pages.

Observed DOI-linked scope

The report analyzes policy-document references that can be linked to scholarly works through DOIs. This observed linkage excludes references that are not DOI-linked. The policy-document input is the project-provided dump_20250211.tar.gz archive dated 11 February 2025. Because the upstream Overton export query and selection criteria are not recoverable from this repository, the 97.6% document-level statistic is conditional on the provided dump and is not an estimate of coverage of the full Overton index.

Share of papers cited by multiple policy documents

This measure is the share of distinct scholarly papers cited by more than one policy document. In the baseline data, 48.1% of unique cited papers are cited by at least two different policy documents.

This value applies only to the observed DOI-linked citation scope.

Share of citations to same-country research

This is a country-level same-country citation measure. For a policy source country, it asks what share of citations point to research whose first-author country is the same country. First-author country means the country of the first author’s affiliated institution, not the author’s nationality.

The country assignment first uses the standardized first-author country mode. If that is unavailable, it uses a single unambiguous two-letter raw country code. Records with no code or conflicting codes remain unassigned. This metric uses only citations assigned a first-author country under that sequential rule. It is therefore better used as a diagnostic than as a simple country ranking.

Linked-sample composition expected share for same-country research cited

The expected share combines the field mix of cited research with each country’s first-author unique-paper share in the DOI-linked article sample. Each article is counted at most once per domain. It is a technical linked-sample baseline. It is not a model-based forecast, a target, or a measure of total global research production in OpenAlex.

Country-assignment denominator

For Korean policy documents, 23,943 of 32,935 DOI-linked citations are assigned a research country; 8,992 (27.3%) remain unassigned and are reported separately. The unassigned share is 28.7% for KDI, 24.2% for KIEP, and 15.4% for other institutions. Placing all unassigned citations outside or inside the U.S. category gives mechanical bounds of 40.1%-67.4% for the U.S. share. These are not model-based intervals. Country-level interpretation should therefore use the assigned-country denominator stated with each metric.

Policy-source composition of Korea’s field gap

The social-science share is 77.9% for research cited by Korean policy documents, 40.3 percentage points above the 37.6% share in the full Overton policy-document sample calculated with the same rule. The share is 23.1% for Korean-authored research cited by policy documents, producing a 54.8-point gap between the two Korea-centred directions. A symmetric two-group decomposition locates 38.2 percentage points in think-tank versus non-think-tank source composition and 16.6 points within those source-type groups.

KDI and KIEP documents account for 71.4% of all Korean policy documents, 71.5% of DOI-citing documents, and 93.5% of DOI-linked citations in the Korean-policy-document direction. Their DOI-citing documents average 15.8 citations, compared with 2.7 for other institutions. Think tanks account for 93.6% in that direction, compared with 12.1% among policy documents citing Korean-authored research. These are descriptive composition results, not causal estimates or intrinsic national preferences.

The social-science gap remains positive under alternative weighting rules: 58.4 percentage points with unique-work weighting, 32.2 points with equal policy-document weighting, and 59.9 points with fractional domain weighting, compared with 54.8 points in the primary analysis. These values answer different descriptive questions and do not form a single uncertainty interval.

Foreign think-tank distribution and citation-profile matches

Korea has the highest think-tank citation share among the 11 report comparison countries. Within Korean think-tank citations, KDI and KIEP account for 99.9%. The eligible foreign comparison contains 748 think tanks across 74 countries. Its median social-science share is 62.6% (range: 1.2-96.0%). KDI and KIEP have shares of 82.1% and 86.0%, placing them at the 91.3rd and 96.4th percentiles.

The top 25 citation-profile matches span 17 countries: four are in the UK, three in the USA, two each in Belgium, France, and Germany, and one each in 12 other countries. Their median social-science share is 78.5%, but this is not an independent replication because the same cited-topic information selects the matches and defines the field shares. Korea’s country-level result combines high institution-level social-science shares with concentration of observed DOI-linked citations in those two institutions. Source-type or citation-profile similarity does not establish equivalent legal status, mandates, or organizational roles.
